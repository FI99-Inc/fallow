## Overall Impression

The thinking is well ahead of the execution and the gap is almost entirely mechanical. The quiz instrument, the "smallest first step" framing, and the willingness to name what will not work are real product insight. Then the build loses two of five surfaces to two one-line null references, renders its central instrument as two identical grey rectangles, and ends the journey by deleting the user's commitment and showing an empty box. Biggest opportunity: this is a wiring problem wearing a design problem's clothes.

## What's Working

1. The quiz's question design is the real product (onboarding.js:5-131). "Which frustration is more tolerable?" (onboarding.js:63-66) isolates delayed-gratification tolerance without naming it. Multi-dimension score weights (onboarding.js:59-60, :85-89) show real thought about construct validity.
2. "Your smallest first step" + "What might not work" (results.html:57-65) pairs every recommendation with an explicit caveat and a minimum viable action, answering the landing page's own quotes. results.js:246-256 derives caveats from dimension distance, not boilerplate.
3. The landing's asymmetric editorial stagger (index.css:527-536) under a clamp(3.5rem, 8vw, 6.5rem) headline at -0.04em produces genuine print cadence — constraints compensated for with scale and rhythm, which the other four surfaces fail to do.

## Questions to Consider

1. If every swipe already refines the DNA (browse.js:322-345), why is the quiz 13 questions long? Could 4 pairwise questions produce a rough profile and the deck do the rest — making the "3 minutes" promise true?
2. What if the reveal were not a chart? The six-slider spectrum is the least specific thing in the product. The insight cards ("You're a Structured Maker", onboarding.js:467) are more memorable and cheaper to render. One enormous Fraunces archetype name, spectrum as optional "see the math"?
3. Who is the share card for — the profile, or the weird specific hobby? "I got matched with competitive dog grooming" is a better tweet than "I am a Structured Maker."
4. Should "Not for me" be a dead end? results.js:191-200 captures pass reasons and throws them away. What if passing for "Too expensive" immediately swapped in a cheaper match?
5. Does "severe, stark, heavy, no soft or playful UI" fit a product about lowering the barrier to starting, for an audience described as embarrassed to be beginners and low on energy?
